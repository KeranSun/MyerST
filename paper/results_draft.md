## R1. Topology-constrained attribution with the Myerson value (Fig. 1, Box 1)

Spatial-omics graph neural networks (GNNs) achieve state-of-the-art performance on tissue domain identification and cell-state prediction, but their predictions are opaque: which genes, spots, and intercellular contacts drive a given prediction is typically interrogated with generic explanation tools — attention weights, integrated gradients, or occlusion — whose faithfulness in spatial settings has never been systematically tested. We argue that the root cause is a mathematical one: every Shapley-family attribution assumes *free coalition formation*, i.e., that any subset of players can jointly contribute. In a tissue, cooperation is constrained by spatial adjacency, and graph neural networks propagate information along exactly those adjacencies. The discrepancy is not cosmetic — on a three-node path graph with coalition worth v(S)=|S|², the Shapley value assigns identical credit (3.00, 3.00, 3.00) to all nodes, erasing the bridge position entirely, whereas the Myerson value assigns (2.67, 3.67, 2.67), making topology visible in credit (Box 1).

MyerST (Fig. 1a) operationalizes Myerson's 1977 communication-game framework for spatial omics. An adapter layer wraps any GNN host (GCN, STAGATE, GAT) behind a minimal forward contract; the Myerson engine estimates values by Monte-Carlo sampling over connected coalitions of the spatial kNN graph, with per-coalition caching and batching. Two properties distinguish the framework from generic explainers. First, **self-auditing**: the component-efficiency axiom yields the exact identity Σφᵢ = v(N)−v(∅), which holds for the exact value and for every individual MC permutation — we verify it to machine precision in all experiments (e.g., 1.2787 = 1.2787 on DLPFC), giving practitioners a built-in correctness check that gradient- and perturbation-based explainers lack. Second, **principled multi-level attribution**: node values decompose onto edges via the fairness axiom (pairwise synergy ψᵢⱼ = v({i,j})−v({i})−v({j})+v(∅)), and the same engine scores genes through feature masking.

## R2. Explanation fidelity is operator- and semantics-dependent (Fig. 2)

We built a redundancy-controlled simulation benchmark (known driver genes, known communicating edges, tunable passenger redundancy) and compared five explainers (Myerson, integrated gradients (IG), occlusion, naive DE, attention weights) under three evaluation protocols: ground-truth recovery (P1), cumulative masking fidelity (P2), and remove-and-retrain ROAR (P3), across three redundancy regimes and multiple simulation seeds.

The central finding is that **fidelity conclusions depend jointly on the evaluation protocol, the target semantics, and the explainer's perturbation operator** — and that naive choices produce confident but arbitrary rankings. Multi-seed replication (a discipline we applied to our own results) exposed this chain explicitly: (i) a raw logit-difference boundary target is degenerate on mixed two-class bands (opposite-class spots cancel, ref ≈ 0), making any game built on it meaningless; (ii) an unsigned or mixed signed margin target has *friendly fire* — an interface spot's activation hurts opposite-class neighbors' margin, so credit flows to interior supporter spots (all methods agree: AUROC ≈ 0.47 for interface ground truth); (iii) the per-side own-class formulation, which matches the ground-truth question, yields clean, stable rankings. Under it, **IG dominates node-level recovery** (AUROC 0.95–0.99 across seeds and regimes), while Myerson's coalition values answer a genuinely different question — *irreplaceability* under spot masking — on which interface spots score low by construction (GCN message passing makes them replaceable by neighbors; Myerson faithfully reports this).

What survives every control, and forms the benchmark's practical conclusions: (i) **attention weights score below random on masking fidelity** (0.17–0.18 vs 0.22–0.24 for random rankings, stable across seeds/regimes), experimentally falsifying a still-common interpretation practice; (ii) **ROAR with accuracy is structurally insensitive on spatial GNNs** — even after removing all ground-truth drivers under extreme sparsification, held-out accuracy remains ≈1.0 (finite-sample offsets + neighbor aggregation guarantee separability); (iii) Myerson's distinctive contributions are the axiomatic ones: exact efficiency self-audit (Σφ = v(N)−v(∅) to machine precision) and principled edge/pathway decomposition (R4/R5), not node ranking.

Methodological safeguards that emerged from this study (E5–E9 lessons, all encoded in the framework): masking-based fidelity is circular for masking-based explainers (perturbation operator matching); domain-mean baselines inject class-centroid signal; probability margins over mixed two-class boundary sets vanish by construction (L5 spots +1, L6 spots −1) and must be class-signed; and in-sample ROAR accuracy is meaningless under overparameterized hosts (train-set accuracy 0.976 after removing all 12 drivers) — held-out evaluation is mandatory.

## R3. Cortical boundary attribution in human DLPFC (Fig. 3)

On the DLPFC 151673 slice (spatialLIBD), MyerST attributed the L5|L6 boundary with a GCN host (accuracy 0.991). Myerson node scores recover the annotated interface with AUROC 0.883 vs 0.993 for IG (we report this comparison honestly: node-level ranking is not where Myerson wins; its contribution is the self-audit, the edge decomposition, and protocol-robust fidelity). The efficiency identity held exactly (Σφ = v(N)−v(∅) = 1.2787).

Gene-level attribution was validated biologically: top-ranked genes are enriched for known layer markers (PCP4, KRT17, RORB among top hits). **Cross-slice robustness (E12)**: across all 12 DLPFC slices (3 donors), hosts reached accuracy 0.990–0.997; PCP4 ranked in the top 20 in 12/12 slices, KRT17 in 11/12, and ≥2 known markers in every slice. Cross-slice attribution Spearman correlation averages 0.314 — we frame this as "core-consistent, tail-heterogeneous": the marker core replicates across donors while tail attributions reflect genuine donor/sectioning heterogeneity, not method noise (same-slice cross-host agreement is much higher at 0.807; Fig. 3e). Host quality matters: a faithful official STAGATE port (GMM clustering ARI 0.522) yields cross-host attribution agreement of 0.807, versus 0.087 for a weak lite host (ARI ~0.30) — attribution quality tracks host quality, a prerequisite rarely checked in spatial XAI (Fig. 3f).

## R4. Tumor–immune communication in breast cancer (Fig. 4)

Our flagship case decomposes the CXCL12–CXCR4 axis on Xenium breast cancer Rep1 (167,782 cells; tumor–immune interface crop of 12,706 cells with balanced Cancer/T-cell/CAF composition). Five iterations of methodological refinement (each motivated by a diagnosed failure — dilution at single-edge granularity, cell-type confounding under whole-cell masking, target–biology mismatch, cell-type-marker swamping, and null-model mispairing) converged on a recipe now encoded in MyerST's CCC module: LR-restricted host (36 signaling genes), location-matched target (T-cell infiltration within 30 µm of cancer), gene-level ligand occlusion, and per-receiver paired nulls.

With this design, the CXCL12→CXCR4 effect on T-cell location is **−1.07 logits (paired t = −38.9, n = 1,806 receivers)** — the negative sign matching the known stromal-retention/exclusion mechanism (CAF-derived CXCL12 traps T cells in stroma; CXCR4 inhibitors release them). The effect is spatially coherent (Fig. 4c: exclusion concentrated in the stromal band), replicates across host seeds (seed 1: −1.02, t = −32.5), and decomposes across sender types: CAFs contribute the largest per-receiver effect (−0.149, t = −22.8), myeloid the highest significance (t = −28.3), with sub-additive redundancy across sources — a more nuanced picture than the canonical CAF-only narrative. The same pipeline finds CD80/CD86→CTLA4 signals (among the first spatial evidence for this axis in breast/lung tumors) and nominates **PTN→SDC4 as a novel candidate** (t = −4.4/−3.1 across seeds): PTN immunomodulation has precedent (Ganguly 2023, via neutrophils), but a PTN–SDC4 role in T-cell spatial positioning is, to our knowledge, unreported. We present it as a hypothesis with explicit limitations (transcriptomic LR inference cannot capture glycan-mediated binding; SDC4 is not PTN's canonical receptor).

## R5. Cross-cohort, cross-platform replication (Fig. 5)

The identical pipeline (no per-cohort code changes) reproduces across four datasets and two platforms: Xenium breast Rep1 (−1.07, t=−38.9), Xenium breast Rep2 (+0.29, t=+18.6), Xenium lung NSCLC (+0.68, t=+5.0), and Visium breast (+1.30, t=+28.5). CXCL12 is the strongest pathway in all four (|t| ≥ 5 throughout), while the **sign varies with cohort infiltration stage** (43% infiltrated T cells in Rep1, where retention/exclusion dominates, vs 6.7% in Rep2, where recruitment dominates) — consistent with the axis's documented dual biology (recruitment, Bleul 1996; exclusion, Feig 2013; COMBAT trial, Bockorny 2020). We therefore claim detectability replication with context-dependent direction, not sign consistency. PTN→SDC4 is significant in three of three testable datasets (Rep1 t=−4.4, Rep2 t=+6.8, Visium t=+12.6), again with context-dependent sign. Visium results carry the caveat of multi-cellular spots and coarse marker-based annotation (77 T spots; host accuracy 1.0) and are presented as supporting, not primary, evidence.
